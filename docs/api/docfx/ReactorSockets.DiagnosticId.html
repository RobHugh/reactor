ReactorSockets

Enum DiagnosticId

Namespace
ReactorSockets
Assembly
ReactorSockets.dll

A closed, permanent enumeration of diagnostic causes with explicit values in reserved blocks — common 1-99, Raw 100-199, datagram session 200-299, stream session 300-399 (ADR 0013) — so a future insertion cannot silently renumber an id a consumer has persisted. There is no None or Unknown member and zero is never used: every DiagnosticRecord has a cause by construction.

public enum DiagnosticId

Fields

AdmissionCapReached = 1

The configured admission cap was hit this Pump with work still waiting to be admitted. No subject. Value0 is the cap, Value1 is the count of Admissions declined, counted as the Reactor's counters count them: once per declining where the work waits out of reach — a listening Link or bound endpoint with a pending connection it left in the OS backlog, never the number of connections left there, a depth unknowable without accepting — and once per piece of work where the Reactor was holding it, whether it then waits or is Discarded.

ArenaExhausted = 2

The frame arena had no room left for a request, regardless of the read-share escape. Subject is the Link that made the request. Value0 is the bytes requested, Value1 is the arena's bytes free at the moment of the request.

CapacityExhausted = 3

Every capacity slot was occupied this Pump with work still waiting to be admitted — a different exhaustion from AdmissionCapReached, reachable because the admission cap can be configured above Capacity. No subject. Value0 is the capacity, Value1 is the count of Admissions declined, counted the same way and with the same limit as AdmissionCapReached's.

LinkFailed = 100

A registered Link failed and closed. Subject is the Link. Value0 carries the platform SocketError as its underlying integer value; Value1 is unused.

DestinationUnreachable = 101

An ICMP port-unreachable (or platform equivalent) was reported for a send. Subject is the Link. Value0 carries the platform SocketError as its underlying integer value; Value1 is unused.

DatagramTruncated = 102

A datagram exceeded the Reactor's scratch buffer and was truncated. Subject is the claimed Source. Value0 is the scratch buffer size in bytes; Value1 is unused.

DatagramMalformed = 200

A datagram was discarded because nothing in it could be attributed to a Peer: wrong length, an unknown control type, a body the declared type cannot fill, padding that was not zero, a session id naming no Session, or a well-formed control reply — a Cookie, an Accept, a Refusal — matching no dial this Reactor is waiting on. That last case is not a parse failure, but it is the same thing to a consumer: bytes arrived, named nobody, and went nowhere. Subject is the claimed Source. Value0 is the bytes received; Value1 is unused.

A remote can produce this at will by sending rubbish, which is exactly why it is a Diagnostic and never an Event: the event stream is the consumer's protocol, and an unauthenticated stranger must not be able to write to it. Warn rather than Error for the same reason — nothing is wrong with this Reactor.

CookieRejected = 201

A Hello carried a cookie this Reactor did not mint, or minted too long ago: a forged or corrupted authenticator, a cookie replayed from a different source address, or one from an epoch older than the previous. Subject is the claimed Source. Value0 is the epoch the cookie offered; Value1 is the epoch current at the moment it was checked.

Warn, never Error: a cookie arriving one epoch after it was minted is the mechanism working, and a Reactor visible to the internet will see forged ones continuously. Raising this to Error would train a consumer to ignore Error.

VersionRefused = 202

A cookie-validated Hello offered a protocol version this Reactor does not speak, and was refused. Subject is the claimed Source. Value0 is the version offered; Value1 is the version this Reactor requires.

Emitted only for a Hello that passed the cookie check, because that is the only Hello a Reactor answers at all — a bare Hello is answered with a Cookie and nothing else, whatever version it claimed.

PeerRefused = 300

A dialler was refused in-protocol during the stream handshake, either because it announced a different protocol version or because every capacity Slot was occupied. Subject is the claimed Source: at refusal time there is no Peer, only a claimed origin. For a capacity refusal, Value0 is this Reactor's capacity and Value1 is unused. For a version refusal, Value0 is the version offered and Value1 is the version this Reactor requires.

Info, not Warn: a version mismatch on an upgrade and an ordinary burst at capacity are both routine, expected outcomes of running this profile, not conditions a consumer needs to act on.

LingerNegotiated = 301

A stream handshake completed and the two ends' announced Linger values were negotiated to their minimum (ADR 0011). Subject is the newly established Peer. Value0 is this end's own Linger in milliseconds; Value1 is the effective (minimum) Linger in milliseconds.

MalformedFraming = 302

An established Peer's Message framing broke this profile's rules and the Session was closed with ProtocolViolation: an oversized or zero-length prefix, or a stream that ended before a prefix or the Message body it named had fully arrived. Subject is the Peer. Value0 is the offending length prefix — 0 for a truncation, where no full prefix was ever read; Value1 is this Reactor's own MaxMessageSize.

Warn: a remote can produce this at will by sending malformed bytes, which is exactly why the Session ends rather than throws, and why this is a Diagnostic rather than an Event on the consumer's own stream.

DataAfterGoodbye = 303

An established Peer sent Message bytes after its own Goodbye had already been received, and the Session was closed with ProtocolViolation (issue #93). Subject is the Peer. Carries no claimed length — ReactorSockets.Session.MessageFramingLegality reaches this outcome before ever reading a prefix, so there is nothing to report but the contradiction itself. Value0 and Value1 are unused.

Warn, for the same reason as MalformedFraming: a remote can produce this at will by ignoring the Goodbye it just sent.

ResumeMatched = 304

A connection presenting a valid resume secret was matched onto the Interrupted Peer whose Slot was reserving the Session it named, and that Session continued over the replacement transport rather than a new one being established (issue #123, ADR 0011). Subject is the resumed Peer — the same PeerId, generation included, the Session had before the interruption, since nothing about a Peer's identity is renewed by a Resume. Value0 is the number of retained outbound bytes the match rewound to the counter the Resume reported; Value1 is unused.

Info: a reconnection is the outcome reserving a Slot exists to produce, not a condition a consumer needs to act on.

Value0 reads 0 until issue #127 supplies the rewind: this slice matches the Resume and reads the counter it reports without acting on it, so no bytes have been rewound yet and 0 is what the match can honestly report.

Numbered 304 rather than the 302 the slice-4 spec named: 302 and 303 were taken by MalformedFraming and DataAfterGoodbye (issues #92 and #106) after that spec was written, and a value published in a closed permanent enum is never renumbered.

ResumeUnmatched = 305

A connection claiming an existing Session was refused rather than resumed. Subject is the claimed Source: a claim that is not matched comes from a party that is by construction not a Peer, so there is no Peer to name, only a claimed origin. Value0 is the number of Interrupted Peers whose reservations the claim was checked against — 0 means nothing was being held at all, which is the reconfiguration path where a once-valid secret arrives after its Linger expired. Value1 is the wire record type the claim arrived as: 3 for a Resume whose secret matched no held Peer, and 1 for a Hello presenting a secret that did match, which re-announces the parameters a Resume has no field for and is refused as a protocol violation.

Warn rather than the Info a version mismatch or a capacity refusal carries: those are routine outcomes of running this profile and a wrong secret is not.

"Unmatched" names the outcome, not the comparison: the claim was not matched onto a Session. The Hello case reaches here having found the secret it announced, and is refused anyway because the record it arrived in re-announces parameters — Value1 is what tells the two apart.

Value0 is reported to this end's own Sink and never to the wire: a refused party is closed on without being told anything, so it learns neither the count nor which of the three refusals it met.

RedialAttempted = 306

A dialling-side Peer began a fresh re-dial attempt after its transport was lost (issue #125, ADR 0011). Subject is the Peer. Value0 is the attempt number, starting at 1 and rising by one per attempt; Value1 is the delay in milliseconds this attempt actually waited before dialling — the jittered figure ReactorSockets.Session.RedialSchedule.NextDelay() drew, so what actually elapsed is observable rather than only the computed figure before jitter.

Info: a re-dial is the ordinary behaviour of a dialling Peer that lost its transport, not a condition a consumer needs to act on.

RedialFailed = 307

A re-dial attempt could not connect (issue #125, ADR 0011). Subject is the Peer, which this never closes — it stays Interrupted and waits out the schedule's next delay, leaving Linger, not an attempt count, the only thing that ever closes it. Value0 carries the platform SocketError as its underlying integer value; Value1 is the attempt number that failed.

Info rather than Warn: under this profile's premise a failed reconnection attempt is an ordinary, expected outcome, and raising it to Warn would train a consumer to ignore Warn.

DeliveryCounterRefused = 308

An established Peer's delivery-counter record (issue #109) claimed to have received more Messages than this end has ever sent it, and the Session was closed with ProtocolViolation (issue #113) — the peer is confirming Messages this end never queued, rather than merely repeating a count it already reported. Subject is the Peer. Value0 is the claimed cumulative count; Value1 is the count of Messages (and, if this end's own Goodbye has already been queued for this Peer, that too) this end has actually appended to this Peer's retention — the largest count a legitimate confirmation could ever sit at or below.

Warn, for the same reason as MalformedFraming: a remote can produce this at will by inflating its own counter.

Numbered 308 rather than the next free 304: ADR 0013's roster, as amended for this slice, reserves 304-307 for the rest of slice 4 (ResumeMatched and ResumeUnmatched, which issue #124 has since taken, then RedialAttempted and RedialFailed), and a value published in a closed permanent enum cannot be moved later to make room.
