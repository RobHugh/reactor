Remarks

A knob carries a default only when that default is derived — from machinery this library owns, from another knob, or from a citable standard. A value that trades the consumer's own resources against their own traffic, and that nothing in this library can derive, is required with no default: MaxMessageSize, RetentionBytes, SendIdleInterval and Linger are all of that kind, and so is Capacity. Guessing any of them would be guessing at the consumer's application.

Values fixed by the wire format are not here at all and cannot be reached from here: the 32-bit acknowledgement width, the 64-entry duplicate window, the four sequence Spaces, zero as the reserved session id, the 20-byte data header and the thirty-second cookie epoch are protocol constants. Changing one changes what a datagram means, which is a version, not a setting.

The memory bill. A Reactor built from these options preallocates, and the whole of it is these three lines:

  • receive: Capacity × 2 × ReceiveWindowFragments × FragmentSize — two of the four Spaces are reliable and reassemble, and each holds a window of whole fragment slots per Peer.
  • send: Capacity × 2 × RetentionBytes — the same two Spaces retain what they have sent until it is acknowledged, per Peer.
  • unreliable queue: Capacity × UnreliableQueueBytes — the bytes of Messages Sent in the two unreliable modes that wait for the next Pump, per Peer.

FragmentSize is in one line and not the other because the window counts packets and retention counts bytes. Neither line has a traffic term: both are allocated at construction and never grow. Each line also carries a few bytes of bookkeeping per fragment slot, which ReceiveBudgetBytes and SendBudgetBytes include exactly — under 2% at the default fragment size.

The frame arena (ArenaSize) is over and above those three lines and is shared across the whole Reactor rather than multiplied by capacity.

One further cost is allocated only under traffic: ordinary ReliableOrdered records waiting behind a gap. It has a ceiling of its own, HeldRecordBudgetBytes, and reaching it refuses further records rather than growing.

MaxMessageSize reaches three places at once: it is announced to the remote as a receive limit, it floors the receive window at the fragments one maximum Message occupies (ReceiveWindowFloor), and it floors the frame arena, because an arena that cannot hold one maximum Message could never deliver one. The limit is enforced as well as announced: a completed Message above it is discarded rather than delivered, with OversizedMessage. Careless enlargement grows memory in two directions rather than one: the window floor is per Peer, so the receive line above rises with it multiplied by capacity and by the Spaces, and the shared arena is pushed up alongside. Retention is the exception this profile makes. RetentionBytes is floored by ResolvedFragmentSize rather than by MaxMessageSize, because a reliable Space retransmits fragment by fragment and needs only one fragment to have something to resend.

Security. The session field on every datagram is a demultiplexing token, not an authenticator: it makes blind off-path injection a 32-bit guess, and does nothing against anyone who has seen one datagram of the Session. This library does not encrypt, and on-path attackers are out of scope (ADR 0009). A consumer who needs confidentiality or integrity against an on-path attacker provides it themselves, inside the Message.

Every check below is deterministic and entirely the caller's, so failures throw at construction — ArgumentOutOfRangeException for a single bad value and ArgumentException for a combination that cannot work — rather than being reported through a Result.

Properties

Endpoints

The endpoints to bind at construction, in the order supplied; possibly empty. Declared once and never rebound (ADR 0014) — a Session's addresses are part of what it is, not something it acquires later. An empty set binds nothing until the first dial, which binds a single wildcard, port-zero socket in the destination's address family. Provenance: empty is the absence of a declaration, not a chosen address — the dial-only socket's family and port are derived from the destination and the OS, never configured.

Entropy

The entropy the cookie secrets, session ids and resume secrets are drawn from. A construction knob in ADR 0012's sense — the consumer's own resource, and a consumer with a hardware source or a policy about which generator may be used is entitled to supply it. It carries a default only because that default is derived rather than guessed. Provenance: the platform's own CSPRNG.

FragmentSize

The bytes a fragment carries, and therefore the unit every buffer here is measured in. Defaults to 1200. Provenance: QUIC's floor beneath the 1280-byte IPv6 minimum MTU (RFC 9000), the one value with citable provenance for surviving an arbitrary path. Must lie between 576 (RFC 791's guaranteed reassembly minimum) and 65 476 (the 65 507-byte maximum UDP payload less the 20-byte Datagram header and the 11-byte Fragment record header). A data Datagram carries at most this many payload bytes plus a 31-byte header, so at the default a Datagram is at most 1231 bytes, which with IPv6 and UDP headers fits the 1280-byte minimum MTU.

HeldRecordBudgetBytes

The most heap, in bytes, a Reactor's ReliableOrdered Spaces can hold in ordinary records waiting behind a gap, across all its Peers: Capacity × 2 × ReceiveWindowFragments × FragmentSize, with each slot's bookkeeping.

Linger

How long a closing Session stays alive to retransmit its Goodbye and absorb what is still in flight. Required, for the same reason as SendIdleInterval: it trades a prompt teardown against a clean one. At most 2^32 - 1 milliseconds (about 49.7 days), the most a handshake may announce.

MaxDatagramsPerPump

The most reads one Pump makes on each bound socket, so a flood cannot keep a Pump from returning. Defaults to ResolvedMaxDatagramsPerPump. Provenance: derived from a full Reactor's worth of traffic, as AdmissionCap is (ADR 0012). At least 1.

MaxMessageSize

The largest Message, in bytes, this Reactor will accept or deliver. Required: it is a statement about the consumer's own protocol that nothing here can derive. It is also the knob that multiplies this profile's memory bill in more than one direction — the class remarks on DatagramSessionOptions say what it floors and what raising it costs.

MaxPeersPerAddress

The most accepted Peers one address may hold at once, so one reachable address cannot occupy every capacity Slot. Defaults to ResolvedMaxPeersPerAddress, one fewer than Capacity. Provenance: derived from capacity, as the largest limit that still keeps one address from holding every Slot (ADR 0014). How many Peers legitimately share an address depends on the NATs and proxies in front of this Reactor, so set it lower when strangers can reach it. At least 1. A value of Capacity or above sets no limit, which is what the default is at a capacity of 1.

MissedHeartbeats

How many consecutive heartbeat intervals may pass in silence before a Peer is declared gone. Defaults to 3. Provenance: RFC 4271's hold time, three times the keepalive interval. Must be at least 2, because a count of 1 makes a single lost datagram fatal and no network is that reliable.

PeerLimitIPv6PrefixLength

How many leading bits of an IPv6 address form the key for MaxPeersPerAddress, so every address in one prefix shares one limit. Defaults to ResolvedPeerLimitIPv6PrefixLength, 64. Provenance: RFC 4291 §2.5.1 fixes the interface identifier of a unicast address at 64 bits, so a /64 is one link (ADR 0014). From 1 to 128. A value of 128 counts each address on its own.

Reactor

The shared L0 budget: capacity, admission cap, arena size, time source and Diagnostic Sink. Required — a Reactor cannot be built without one, and its Capacity has no default of its own.

ReceiveBudgetBytes

The receive line of the memory bill in bytes, as a long so that a combination too large to allocate is a value this can report rather than an overflow it silently wraps through.

ReceiveWindowFloor

The resolved receive window: ReceiveWindowFragments if set, otherwise the fragments one maximum Message occupies.

ReceiveWindowFragments

The fragments each reliable Space may have outstanding per Peer. Defaults to exactly the number a maximum Message occupies. Provenance: derived from MaxMessageSize and FragmentSize — ADR 0004's deadlock floor rather than a performance choice: a window narrower than one whole Message can never deliver one, so it can never advance, so the sender can never send the rest. This resolved value is also the inbound bound a declared Fragment fragmentCount is checked against: a set larger than the window can never be reassembled, since there is no slot to hold every one of its pieces at once, so DatagramSessionReactor closes it before any is held.

ResolvedFragmentSize

The resolved fragment size: FragmentSize if set, otherwise 1200.

ResolvedMaxDatagramsPerPump

The resolved per-socket read limit: MaxDatagramsPerPump if set, otherwise one receive window for every Space of every Peer, plus every handshake the admission cap allows in one Pump.

ResolvedMaxPeersPerAddress

The resolved per-address limit: MaxPeersPerAddress if set, otherwise one fewer than Capacity, and at least 1.

ResolvedMissedHeartbeats

The resolved missed-heartbeat count: MissedHeartbeats if set, otherwise 3.

ResolvedPeerLimitIPv6PrefixLength

The resolved IPv6 grouping length: PeerLimitIPv6PrefixLength if set, otherwise 64.

ResolvedReceiveWindowFragments

The resolved receive window: ReceiveWindowFragments if set, otherwise ReceiveWindowFloor.

ResolvedUnreliableQueueBytes

UnreliableQueueBytes with its default applied, clamped to MaxValue.

RetentionBytes

The bytes each reliable Space retains per Peer for retransmission, until what it holds is acknowledged. Required: this is the consumer trading their memory against their tolerance for stalling on loss, and the right answer depends on their traffic, not on anything here.

RetentionSlots

The uniform retention slots one reliable Space holds per Peer: RetentionBytes divided by ResolvedFragmentSize, truncating any remainder. Stays derived from the byte bound rather than a knob of its own (ADR 0012) so it cannot drift from RetentionBytes when the fragment size changes. ReactorSockets.DatagramSessionOptions.Validate() already requires retention to hold at least one fragment, so this is always at least 1.

SendBudgetBytes

The send line of the memory bill in bytes, reported the same way: RetentionSlots whole fragment slots per reliable Space per Peer, each priced at its payload plus the 15 bytes of bookkeeping that records what it holds. Exact, like the receive line, so it can be below Capacity × 2 × RetentionBytes when retention is not a whole number of fragments.

SendIdleInterval

How long a Session may go without sending before it sends a Ping instead. Required: shorter costs bandwidth on an idle link, longer costs time to notice a dead Peer, and only the consumer knows which they would rather spend. There is no option to disable this: every Established Peer that has sent no data datagram for this long is always sent one seven-byte control record, whatever else it did or did not receive. At most 2^32 - 1 milliseconds (about 49.7 days), the most a handshake may announce.

UnreliableQueueBudgetBytes

The unreliable-queue line of the memory bill in bytes: Capacity × ResolvedUnreliableQueueBytes, one region per Peer, allocated at construction.

UnreliableQueueBytes

The most bytes one Peer may have queued for Unreliable and UnreliableSequenced between Pumps, counting each Message's payload plus its 7-byte record header. A Send that would go past it returns SendQueueFull. null means RetentionSlots × (ResolvedFragmentSize + 7): as many full unreliable Messages as one reliable Space retains Fragments. Provenance: derived from RetentionBytes and FragmentSize, so the unreliable queue holds as much as a reliable Space does. Must be at least ResolvedFragmentSize + 7, one full unreliable Message.
